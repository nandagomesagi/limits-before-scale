# Definitions

These definitions support the public conceptual research layer. They are intentionally operational enough to reduce ambiguity without describing proprietary product mechanics.

## System
A structure composed of interacting components, dependencies, interfaces, and boundaries.

## Scale
An increase in capacity, reach, throughput, capability, users, resources, or complexity of a system.

## Limit
A boundary that constrains the operating range, authority, load, behavior, or expansion of a system.

## Constraint
A condition that restricts what a system can currently support, execute, or sustain.

A constraint may be physical, technical, operational, cognitive, economic, regulatory, temporal, or governance-related.

## Architecture
The arrangement of components, dependencies, interfaces, controls, and boundaries that determines how a system operates.

## Amplification
An increase in the reach, speed, capability, influence, or output of an existing structure or process.

Amplification does not imply improvement; it can amplify both effective and ineffective structures.

## Control
A mechanism that keeps system behavior within defined operating boundaries or enables intervention when those boundaries are approached or exceeded.

## Operating Boundary
A defined range within which a system is expected to function under stated conditions without exceeding accepted limits.

## Stability
The ability of a system to continue operating within its defined boundaries under expected variation and load.

## Noise
Information, behavior, interaction, or system output that reduces signal quality, decision clarity, coordination, or reliable execution relative to the system's objective.

## Dependency
A component, condition, resource, or prior state that must exist or remain functional for another component or outcome to operate as intended.

## Complexity
The number and interaction density of relevant components, dependencies, states, and control relationships within a system.

## Core distinction

**Limits Before Scale** asks whether boundaries and controls remain adequate as scale and complexity increase.

**Architecture Before Amplification** asks what underlying structure will be amplified before amplification is treated as beneficial or sustainable.
