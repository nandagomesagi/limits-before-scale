 Limits Before Scale:
A Design Principle for Complex Systems

Nanda Gomes 

Modern technological systems increasingly operate at global scale. However,
scaling complex systems without clear structural limits often leads to
instability, noise amplification, and unpredictable behavior.

This paper proposes the Limits Before Scale principle: scale amplifies the
structure of a system, therefore limits must be defined before scaling.

We present a simple conceptual model describing the relationship between
system structure, limits, and stability. We also outline collapse dynamics
that occur when complexity grows faster than control mechanisms.

The principle applies to artificial intelligence systems, digital platforms,
organizations, and infrastructure networks. By defining limits as structural
components rather than reactive controls, systems can scale while preserving
stability and coherence.

Complex systems tend to degrade when scale amplifies complexity faster
than governance, control, or signal preservation mechanisms.

Examples include large digital platforms, financial systems, and emerging
AI infrastructures.

Scale amplifies structure.

If scale exceeds limits → instability.
